# What the best engineer portfolios cover, and the pattern to follow

## The short version

Recruiters look at your résumé and LinkedIn first. They decide in about 30 seconds, and mostly on job titles, company names and dates. The site comes after that. It breaks ties and gives the interviewer things to ask you about.

So the best sites do three things. They say who you are and what you do on the first screen. They show 3 to 5 pieces of work, each with a clear result. Then they make it easy to get the résumé and send you an email.

**How sure are we**

| | |
|---|---|
| Facts | Recruiters spend a median of 31 seconds per résumé ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/are-recruiters-better-than-a-coin-flip-at-judging-resumes)). About 80% of a first scan goes to name, titles, companies and dates ([TheLadders, via Recruiter.com](https://www.recruiter.com/i/theladders-reveals-research-reporting-that-resumes-spend-six-seconds-with-a-recruiter/)). | Guesses | Nobody has measured how long reviewers spend on an engineer's site. The "60 second" timings in checklists are opinions. |
| Not tested yet | How your film behaves on a mid-range phone, with a thumb, and with scripts blocked. |

## 2. The common order, top to bottom

1. **Who you are.** Name, role, one plain sentence. Often the current company too.
2. **The work.** Experience, selected projects, or both. Brittany and Henry put Experience first. Paco, Emil and Dennis put projects first.
3. **The exits to depth.** "View full résumé", "View full archive", or links to longer write-ups.
4. **Writing**, if they have it.
5. **A short About**, or a personal "Now" note.
6. **Contact.** Email and a few links, usually repeated in the footer.

The rule under all of them: proof comes before the life story. A bio placed before any work was flagged as a red flag in two sources.

## 3. What the best do that average ones don't

1. **They say where they work and what they built in the first two sentences.** Paco names his current company and then one line on what he built at the last one. No slogan. ([paco.me](https://paco.me), [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski))
2. **They pick a few pieces and push the rest away.** Brittany shows 4 projects and links a table of about 46 more. Henri splits "Selected works" from a full "Gallery". ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com), [henriheymans.com](https://henriheymans.com))
3. **They put one hard number on each item.** Brittany's project cards show install and star counts. Henry's job bullets show users and percent fewer API calls. ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com), [henryheffernan.com](https://henryheffernan.com))
4. **They use two layers.** A home page you can scan in 30 seconds, and a long write-up one click away. Emil's toast essay opens with the download number, then walks through each decision. ([emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski))
5. **Every entry uses the same small template.** Brittany: date, title and company, 2 to 3 lines, tech tags. Henri: one fact row per project with type, year, credit, result and stack. Easy to scan. ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com), [henriheymans.com](https://henriheymans.com))
6. **They say what was theirs and credit the team.** Dennis lists collaborators on each case. Rauno ends his deep write-up with thanks to teammates. It reads as ownership and teamwork at once. ([dennissnellenberg.com](https://dennissnellenberg.com), [rauno.me](https://rauno.me))
7. **They turn the site itself into proof.** Henry writes a short case study on how his 3D site works and links the code. Bruno has a "behind the scenes" panel. Brittany has a one-line colophon (a note on how the site was built). ([henryheffernan.com](https://henryheffernan.com), [bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com))
8. **Contact is one click and has no form.** Paco gives an email in one sentence. Rauno has a copy-email button. ([paco.me](https://paco.me), [rauno.me](https://rauno.me))

## 4. What to skip

| Skip this | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Click gates and preloaders before any content | Henri, Henry and Bruno all make you click first. Bruno took about 45 seconds to load in testing. Reviewers skim fast. | [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/), [standout.work](https://standout.work/blog/portfolio-for-senior-software-engineers) |
| Text that only appears after a scroll animation | On a slow phone or with scripts blocked, the reviewer sees blank space. Pinned scroll sections fight a phone thumb. | [rationalgo.ai](https://rationalgo.ai/resources/app-builder/prompts/animated-portfolio-website-prompt-scroll-effects) |
| "Coming soon" sections, dead demos, an old résumé | An empty or stale section counts against you. | [Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41656015), [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/) |
| Skill bars, logo walls, long tool lists | Says nothing you can check. Put tech tags on each job instead. | [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/), [standout.work](https://standout.work/blog/portfolio-for-senior-software-engineers) |
| Filler like "passionate about..." | Generic copy was a turn-off for hiring leads. | [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think), [Opendoors](https://blog.opendoorscareers.com/p/how-recruiters-and-hiring-managers-actually-look-at-your-portfolio) |
| Showing everything you ever built | A highlight reel beats an index. | [Josh Comeau](https://joshwcomeau.com/effective-portfolio/) |
| A contact form | Extra step for a busy reviewer. You already have an orphaned one, so this is easy to drop. | [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/) |
| Blurred real screenshots of private work | Blur can be undone. Draw your own simple diagram instead. | [hassanjaved.work](https://www.hassanjaved.work/blog/working-under-nda-freelance-engineer-guide) |
| Unsourced stats like "86% of employers visit portfolios" | They could not be traced to any real survey. | [theinterviewguys (example)](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=10630) |

## 5. Your section list, below the film

This uses the facts from your résumé (`resume/resume.html`). I also checked what the `redesign-signal` branch has today, so each section says what to keep and what to change.

**A rule for the film itself (not a section).** Your name, your role and a "skip to work" link should be visible while the film plays. Henry keeps a small name badge in the corner during his 3D intro. Keep all text in the HTML. With reduced motion turned on, show the end frame. Your branch already puts the name, role and buttons in the first chapter, which is good.

### 1. Intro (2 to 3 sentences, plus 4 links)

- One plain line, using Emil's "Today / Before" pattern. For example: *Software engineer, 5 years in TypeScript. Today I build the web side of a crowdfunding platform used in 100+ countries, and automate the work behind it. Before that, its mobile app and its first test automation.*
- Links: email, résumé, LinkedIn, GitHub.
- Keep your four-number strip (5 years, 100+ countries, under 5 minutes, about 7 hours a week). Each number should link to the case it came from.
- **Fix:** the branch says "Six years" in one place and 5 years in another. Your résumé says 5. Pick one.

### 2. Selected work (3 case studies, 4 at most)

This is the section hiring managers came for. Each case gets the same template:
- **Title that states the result.**
- **Fact row:** role, team, stack, when, result.
- **Problem** (2 lines). **What I did** (3 to 4 lines). **One key decision and why.** **Result**, with the number. **What I would do next.**
- Use your own simple diagram, not screenshots of internal tools.

The three I would pick, because together they tell one story about automation:

1. **Current web role (2026): a days-long approval process now takes minutes.** Say it in general terms: you automated a manual approval process that crossed several departments. One workflow now connects the website to the payment, customer and task tools. Turnaround went from days to minutes. No vendor names, no internal steps. Your branch has no case for this yet, and it is your newest and strongest story.
2. **QA automation (2025): breakages found in under five minutes, not hours.** You built the company's first Playwright suite with reusable helpers others extended. It ran every five minutes as a live check and sent an alert on failure. Your branch already has this.
3. **Aurem (2022 to 2024): release builds that run themselves, 7 hours a week back.** You automated the mobile app builds with GitHub Actions. Your branch already has this.

Optional 4th: **Mobile role: rebuilt core screens of a React Native app used in 100+ countries**, each feature shipped end to end with tests. Only add it if you can show it without private screens. Otherwise it lives in Experience as one line.

**Change:** move AI Interview Coach out of Selected work and into Projects. It is a prototype, and mixing it with production work makes both weaker. Bruno and Rauno keep real work and experiments apart for this reason.

### 4. Projects (side builds, one line each)

- **Nidham.** Agentic planner for Muslims with ADHD. MVP built end to end in 48 hours. Three agents turn a brain-dump into a plan that works around the five daily prayers. Link the case study.
- **AI Interview Coach.** Prototype of a real-time voice interviewer with a lip-synced video avatar. Label it as a prototype.
- **This site.** One short note on how the film works, plus a link to the code if you are happy to make it public.
- **Fix:** your branch shows a "Coming soon" tag on the Nidham demo. Either ship the demo or show a short screen recording. Stale "coming soon" content is a named turn-off.

### 5. Writing (only if you publish something before launch)

If you can write 1 or 2 posts, this is the strongest route to recruiter messages in the evidence. Good first topics: how you built a company's first Playwright suite, or how the stone film works. If you will not write anything soon, leave this section out completely. An empty one hurts.

### 6. About (2 to 5 lines, after the work)

Your branch copy is already good: you own the parts next to the feature, you work remote in EMEA, you speak five languages. Add one personal line if you like. No photo.

### 7. Contact

- **Fix:** put a plain email first. Today the branch offers LinkedIn, the résumé and GitHub, but no email. Email is the easiest path for a busy reviewer.
- Keep the one-line availability note ("open to full-time roles...").
- A one-line colophon in the footer.

**Leave out:** a skills section (tags on each role do this job), testimonials unless they are real, awards, services, a click gate, a contact form.

**On the order (my judgment, not proven):** I put Selected work before Experience because the intro already carries your titles and company. Recruiters get their scan data on the first screen, and hiring managers reach the case studies fast. Brittany does it the other way round, and that also works. Either is fine, as long as the titles and dates are visible early.
